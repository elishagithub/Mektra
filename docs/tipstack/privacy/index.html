TipStack Privacy Policy

Last updated: 18 October 2026

TipStack is a timing and stacking game published by Ark Code Solutions. Contact: sureshc5551@gmail.com.

Gameplay and preferences

Your best score, sound settings and chosen nickname are saved locally on your device. Gameplay does not require a personal account.

Leaderboards

Unity Authentication assigns an anonymous player identifier. Unity Leaderboards receives that identifier, your submitted best score and optional public nickname to display rankings. Your public nickname and score may be visible to other players. Please do not use private information in your nickname. These services may process technical information needed to provide and secure their service.

Advertising

TipStack includes the Unity Ads SDK. When advertising is enabled, Unity may process device identifiers, IP address, approximate location, device and app information, and ad interactions to deliver ads, measure performance and prevent fraud. Advertising availability and privacy choices depend on your region and the released version. See Unity’s game player and app user privacy policy for its data practices and choices.

Sharing

Sharing is initiated by you using your device’s sharing tools. On Mac, this can include an image of your tower and score; on Android, score text is shared. A skyline image may be saved in app storage. We do not automatically publish your results to social networks.

Children

TipStack is intended for players aged 13 and older and is not primarily directed at children. Do not provide personal information through a public nickname.

Retention and deletion

Local data can be removed by clearing the app’s storage or uninstalling it. To request deletion of leaderboard data, email the contact above with your public nickname and any player identifier available to you. We may ask for information needed to locate and verify your record. Unity retains information according to its own service policies and legal obligations.

Security and changes

Connected services use encrypted network connections. No method of transmission or storage is completely secure. We will update this page when these practices change.
